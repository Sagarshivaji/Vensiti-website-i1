Contrast problems in the current screens
Orange body text on navy (Jobs hero subhead) measures under 4.5:1 — use white at 80% instead, keep orange for headline words only. The orange "Contact US" button uses white label text in some screens and navy in others; navy on orange passes, white does not. Pick navy and apply everywhere.

07

Page skeleton

Every one of your nine screens follows the same five-part rhythm.

navy hero
01 Hero Eyebrow, two-line headline (white + orange), one-sentence subhead, up to two pills, illustration or photo right.
02 Substance The card grid or checklist that answers "what exactly do you do". White band.
03 Process Numbered steps on grey. Last step is orange. Appears on Hire Talent, Jobs, How We Work.
04 Proof Guarantee, testimonials, numbers, or the results cards. Whatever earns the ask.
Get in Touch →
05 CTA band Blue band, question headline, single orange pill. One per page, directly above the footer.
Vertical rhythm
Section padding 96px desktop / 64 tablet / 48 mobile. Container 1280 max with 48px gutters. Card grid gap 24. Cards never exceed three columns on service pages, two on comparison pages. The Contact page currently has a large empty band under the form — that is a missing section, not intentional whitespace.
08

Imagery & icons

illustrated tech diagram
Keep — service heroes
The DevOps, Testing and Contact heroes use dark UI-mockup illustrations. They never look fake because they aren't pretending to be photographs. Use this style for all four service pages.
real photo needed
Replace — the stock photos
The Hire Talent hero photo (woman at a polling station with US flags) has nothing to do with hiring engineers. The Home insights cards use a dog at a laptop and a "LOOKING FOR A JOB?" sign. These read as filler and cost trust.
Icon chip · 46px, radius 12
Blue-50 square, 20px outline glyph, 1.7px stroke, one set only. Never coloured, never filled, never larger than 46px.
Two things to remove before launch
The "Trusted by" bar on Home shows Google, CNN, Apple, Burger King and Harley-Davidson. Unless every one of those is a real, contractually referenceable client, it has to go — it is the single largest legal and credibility risk on the site. Replace it with the industry pills you already have. Second: the three leadership photos on About are stock portraits under "[FILL IN Name]". Ship that section as text-only bios until real photos exist.
